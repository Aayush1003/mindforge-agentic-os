# Open Problems in Math and Physics

This section is a learning and mission roadmap for MindForge: AI should help humans understand difficult problems, reproduce known results, identify promising approaches, and communicate uncertainty clearly.

## What "solve" means here

An answer should include:

- A precise statement of the problem
- Definitions and assumptions
- Known partial results
- Verification and reproducibility criteria
- A clear distinction between evidence, conjecture, and proof
- Ethical and societal benefits if solved
- What still remains for humans to decide or establish

AI can accelerate exploration, search, calculation, and explanation. It cannot replace a verified proof, an independent experiment, or human scientific judgment.

## Millennium Prize Problems

The Clay Mathematics Institute's Millennium Prize Problems are seven famous mathematical problems. A correct solution must meet rigorous mathematical standards. The Poincaré conjecture is resolved; the remaining six are generally considered unresolved.

### 1. Riemann Hypothesis

**Problem:** All non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part 1/2.

**Why it matters:** It would improve understanding of prime-number distribution and support stronger results in number theory, cryptography research, and computation.

**Human question:** Can a person or human-led team find a proof that satisfies the standards of the mathematical community?

### 2. P versus NP

**Problem:** Determine whether every problem whose proposed solution can be checked in polynomial time can also be solved in polynomial time.

**Why it matters:** It concerns the limits of efficient computation, algorithms, verification, optimization, and AI.

**Human question:** Can humanity prove P = NP or P ≠ NP, rather than infer the answer from limited examples?

### 3. Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness

**Problem:** Establish whether solutions of the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations always exist and remain smooth for all time under appropriate conditions, or whether singularities can develop.

**Why it matters:** It describes fluid motion used in engineering, weather, medicine, aviation, and climate science.

**Human question:** Can rigorous mathematics close the gap between numerical evidence and a general proof?

### 4. Yang–Mills and the mass gap

**Problem:** For quantum Yang–Mills theory, prove existence in four-dimensional spacetime and show that there is a positive mass gap.

**Why it matters:** It is central to understanding the mathematical foundations of particle physics and quantum fields.

**Human question:** Can mathematics describe the theory rigorously in a way that also matches observed particle behavior?

### 5. Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture

**Problem:** Relate the rank of an elliptic curve to the behavior of its associated L-function at a special point.

**Why it matters:** It connects arithmetic, geometry, cryptography, and modern number theory.

**Human question:** Can humans prove the conjecture broadly enough and validate every necessary definition and edge case?

### 6. Hodge conjecture

**Problem:** Determine whether certain topological classes on complex projective varieties are representable by algebraic cycles.

**Why it matters:** It links geometry, topology, and algebra and could reveal deep structure in mathematical spaces.

**Human question:** Can human researchers construct a general proof and verify that it handles all required varieties?

## Major physics questions that need human understanding

These are active research questions, not Millennium Prize Problems unless stated otherwise.

- **Quantum gravity:** Reconcile quantum field theory with general relativity; explain the behavior of spacetime at extreme scales.
- **Dark matter and dark energy:** Identify what they are and explain their gravitational and cosmic effects.
- **Matter–antimatter asymmetry:** Explain why the observable universe contains more matter than antimatter.
- **Quantum measurement:** Determine which interpretations make testable predictions and whether an observer-independent formulation is possible.
- **Unification:** Determine whether all fundamental interactions have a deeper shared framework.
- **Turbulence:** Build stronger predictive and mathematical understanding of nonlinear fluid flows.
- **Origin of consciousness:** Develop testable hypotheses connecting physical brain activity to subjective experience without unsupported claims.

## Human–AI workflow

1. **Human defines the problem.** A claim must be precise and falsifiable or proof-ready.
2. **AI explores representations.** Search literature, generate candidates, inspect edge cases, and suggest counterexamples.
3. **Human verifies the claim.** Check reasoning, assumptions, citations, calculations, and independent sources.
4. **The community reproduces it.** Other researchers should be able to repeat simulations or evaluate proofs.
5. **Keep the result honest.** Label conjectures, unresolved questions, and numerical evidence clearly.

The goal is human judgment amplified by AI—not automated certainty. Progress is valuable even when it is a clearer formulation, a stronger partial result, a new counterexample, or a reproducible experiment.
