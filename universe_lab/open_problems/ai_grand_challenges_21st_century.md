# AI-Assisted Grand Challenges for the 21st Century

This roadmap describes major scientific and technical problems that AI can help humans tackle over roughly the next three years (2027–2029), and over the broader 21st century. It covers mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, medicine, Earth systems, engineering, computation, cognition, and civilization-scale challenges.

AI is a research amplifier, not an oracle. A scientific solution is not established by a fluent explanation or a confident prediction: it requires precise definitions, experimental or mathematical verification, independent reproduction, uncertainty quantification, and human judgment.

## How to read this roadmap

- **Near-term priority:** A problem AI could materially accelerate within three years.
- **Longer horizon:** A result likely requiring a century-scale research program or a transformative theoretical breakthrough.
- **Measurable target:** A concrete validation goal.
- **What AI can do:** A useful role for current and emerging systems.
- **What humans must do:** The decisions, experiments, proofs, ethics, and oversight that cannot be delegated responsibly.

## 1. Mathematics

### 1.1 Automated theorem discovery and proof

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Develop AI systems that discover useful conjectures, propose proof strategies, translate informal reasoning into machine-checked proofs, and identify invalid steps.  **Near-term target:** Reach reliable proof generation and repair across large corpora of formal mathematics, while minimizing hallucinated dependencies.

**Measurable outcome:** Machine-checkable proof libraries, reproducible theorem-discovery benchmarks, and independent expert review.

**Human role:** Set axioms, judge mathematical significance, verify assumptions, and accept theorems.

### 1.2 Climate-scale optimization and dynamical proofs

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Prove stability bounds and derive certified guarantees for numerical models used in turbulent flow, epidemiology, climate, and control systems.

**AI role:** Search candidate inequalities, reduce symbolic expressions, find counterexamples, and help translate proofs between languages.

### 1.3 Hard problems in algorithmic lower bounds

**Timeframe:** Longer horizon.  **Goal:** Resolve major barriers involving P versus NP, quantum speedups, circuit complexity, communication complexity, and unconditionally secure cryptography.

**Measurable outcome:** New theorems with assumptions stated explicitly and peer-reviewed verification.

### 1.4 Foundations of trustworthy reasoning

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Create systems that distinguish proof, conjecture, simulation, analogy, and unsupported claims; output evidence graphs with traceable citations.

**Measurable outcome:** Lower rates of fabricated citations and invalid logical steps across independently evaluated tasks.

### 1.5 Mathematics for explainable and certified AI

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Develop rigorous methods to bound model behavior, certify formally specified properties, detect distribution shift, and reason about the safety of learned systems.

**Measurable outcome:** Certified performance on held-out distributions, reproducible counterexamples, and proofs or conservative bounds for meaningful operational properties.

## 2. Fundamental physics

### 2.1 Quantum gravity

**Timeframe:** Longer horizon.  **Goal:** Build a mathematically consistent theory connecting general relativity and quantum mechanics, with predictions that can distinguish candidate models.

**Near-term AI contribution:** Compare models, derive consequences, search experimental signatures, and analyze large theory and detector datasets.

**Measurable outcome:** Testable predictions for black holes, the early universe, gravitational waves, or quantum-spacetime effects.

### 2.2 Dark matter and dark energy

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority for progress; longer horizon for identification.  **Goal:** Determine whether one or several particles, fields, modifications of gravity, or observational effects explain the missing matter and accelerated expansion.

**AI role:** Model joint datasets, optimize searches, detect rare events, and control systematic errors.

**Measurable outcome:** Reproducible particle detections or statistically decisive observations that survive independent blind analysis.

### 2.3 The Standard Model and beyond

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Explain neutrino masses, matter–antimatter asymmetry, possible supersymmetry, new particles, and tensions among precision measurements.

**Measurable outcome:** Falsifiable predictions and measurements in particle colliders, neutrino observatories, and precision experiments.

### 2.4 Quantum foundations and measurement

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Develop observer-independent interpretations or formulations that make distinct, testable predictions rather than philosophical restatements.

**Measurable outcome:** Experiments constraining alternative models of collapse, hidden variables, or quantum measurement.

### 2.5 Turbulence and nonlinear fluid dynamics

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Improve prediction and reduced models for turbulent flows across atmospheres, oceans, engines, blood vessels, fusion reactors, and galaxies.

**Measurable outcome:** Uncertainty-bounded models that outperform baselines under out-of-distribution conditions and are robust to sparse data.

### 2.6 Controlled fusion

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Sustain stable, net-energy fusion with durable materials and economically credible operation.

**Measurable outcome:** Net output over a meaningful operating cycle, validated component lifetime, and reproducible plant-level economics.

**Safety requirement:** Publicly verifiable results, radiation controls, and independent facility review.

### 2.7 Cosmological origin and fate

**Timeframe:** Longer horizon.  **Goal:** Explain the initial conditions of the universe, inflation, vacuum energy, cosmic anisotropies, and possible long-term outcomes.

**Measurable outcome:** Predictions discriminating among models using cosmic microwave background, gravitational-wave, galaxy-survey, and supernova data.

### 2.8 Quantum advantage

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Identify real applications where fault-tolerant quantum computers outperform the best classical alternatives.

**Measurable outcome:** End-to-end demonstrations with independent verification, resource estimates, and no overstated “quantum supremacy” claims.

### 2.9 Fundamental constants and precision metrology

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Improve measurements and tests of whether physical constants and fundamental symmetries vary across space and time.

**Measurable outcome:** Independent precision measurements with falsifiable constraints on possible variations and controlled systematic uncertainties.

### 2.10 High-pressure and extreme-state matter

**Timeframe:** Longer horizon.  **Goal:** Understand matter under extreme temperature, density, pressure, magnetic fields, and rapid evolution.

**Measurable outcome:** Predictive equations of state independently reproduced by experiments, observations, and simulations.

## 3. Chemistry

### 3.1 Predictive molecular simulation

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Predict reaction rates, selectivity, phase behavior, spectra, stability, and solvation with explicit confidence bounds.

**Measurable outcome:** Blind predictions that generalize beyond training chemistry and improve when confronted with new experimental results.

### 3.2 Inverse molecular design

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Design molecules from required properties while satisfying synthesisability, toxicity, cost, stability, and environmental constraints.

**Measurable outcome:** Successfully synthesized candidates with independently replicated performance.

### 3.3 General reaction prediction

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Predict major and minor products, side reactions, conditions, kinetics, and failed experiments across organic, inorganic, polymer, electrochemical, and biochemical chemistry.

**Measurable outcome:** High accuracy on new reaction classes and preservation of known mechanistic constraints.

### 3.4 Green chemistry and circular materials

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Replace scarce, toxic, energy-intensive, or persistent chemicals and materials with scalable, safe alternatives.

**Measurable outcome:** Lower lifecycle emissions and toxicity, not just improved isolated-process yields.

### 3.5 Catalysts for carbon-free and low-carbon processes

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Discover durable catalysts for hydrogen production, CO₂ conversion, nitrogen fixation, water splitting, and fuel synthesis.

**Measurable outcome:** High activity and selectivity under realistic contaminants, pressure, temperature, and long operating periods.

### 3.6 Room-temperature superconductivity

**Timeframe:** Longer horizon.  **Goal:** Discover and reproduce practical superconducting materials at ambient conditions or provide rigorously established limits on their possibility.

**Measurable outcome:** Replicated zero-resistance and magnetic measurements at stated conditions, by independent laboratories.

### 3.7 Stable and reversible batteries

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Improve energy density, charging, safety, abundance, recyclability, and lifetime beyond lithium-ion incumbents.

**Measurable outcome:** Independent cycle-life, abuse-testing, and material-supply validation under realistic use.

### 3.8 Origin of life and prebiotic chemistry

**Timeframe:** Longer horizon.  **Goal:** Identify plausible pathways from simple chemistry to replicating, compartmentalized, evolving systems.

**Measurable outcome:** Reproducible chemical systems with heredity, metabolism-like behavior, and environmental plausibility.

### 3.9 Safe-by-design chemistry

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Predict toxicity, persistence, ecotoxicity, flammability, and endocrine disruption early enough to replace hazardous candidates before industrial scale-up.

**Measurable outcome:** Prospective predictions validated by new data and lower lifecycle harm across diverse exposure scenarios.

## 4. Biology

### 4.1 Complete protein and biomolecular dynamics

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Predict structure, conformational ensembles, interactions, post-translational modifications, and function across proteins and nucleic acids.

**Measurable outcome:** Prospective experiments predicting effects of mutations or perturbations before measurement.

### 4.2 General systems biology

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Create interpretable models linking genes, proteins, metabolites, cells, tissues, organisms, and environment.

**Measurable outcome:** Causal, perturbation-specific predictions with calibrated uncertainty and testing in new biological contexts.

### 4.3 Genome editing safety and control

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Improve specificity and outcome prediction for editing somatic and, where legally permitted, research-only germline systems.

**Measurable outcome:** Reduced off-target effects, robust long-term follow-up, and independent genomic validation.

**Hard constraint:** No autonomous deployment of heritable editing or engineered biological agents outside approved, governed research.

### 4.4 Aging and regenerative medicine

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority for interventions; longer horizon for full control.  **Goal:** Understand hallmarks of aging, slow functional decline, and restore damaged tissues without uncontrolled growth.

**Measurable outcome:** Clinically meaningful improvements in healthy lifespan with acceptable safety in randomized trials.

### 4.5 Cancer control and early detection

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Detect cancers earlier, map tumor evolution, predict resistance, and match treatments to individual biology.

**Measurable outcome:** Reduced mortality or morbidity in prospective, diverse clinical trials, not only biomarker classification accuracy.

### 4.6 Neurodegenerative diseases

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Identify disease mechanisms and treatments for Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, ALS, and other neurodegenerative conditions.

**Measurable outcome:** Durable cognitive or motor benefits validated across independent cohorts and real-world settings.

### 4.7 Antibiotic resistance

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Discover and test new antibiotics, alternatives, diagnostics, and stewardship systems that reduce resistance.

**Measurable outcome:** Effective human or animal treatments against resistant infections with preserved tissue safety and favorable resistance trajectories.

### 4.8 Pandemic prediction and response

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Combine genomic, environmental, clinical, and mobility data to detect outbreaks early and design interventions that minimize harm.

**Measurable outcome:** Better forecasts, validated intervention decisions, faster diagnostics, and transparent false-alarm rates.

**Privacy requirement:** Data minimization, consent, differential privacy, equitable access, and strict limits on surveillance uses.

### 4.9 Microbiome and host health

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Identify stable causal links between microbial communities and health, then design safe interventions.

**Measurable outcome:** Replicated clinical effects across environments and populations, not correlations alone.

### 4.10 Consciousness and cognition

**Timeframe:** Longer horizon.  **Goal:** Develop testable models linking neural dynamics to perception, memory, attention, and subjective experience.

**Measurable outcome:** Predictions about neural activity, behavior, disorders, or anesthesia that distinguish among competing theories.

### 4.11 Artificial life and synthetic organisms

**Timeframe:** Near-term safety and design work.  **Goal:** Create predictable biological parts and organisms for medicines, materials, agriculture, and environmental cleanup.

**Measurable outcome:** Contained, documented systems with fail-safe controls, environmental risk assessment, and independent verification.

### 4.12 Organogenesis and whole-organ development

**Timeframe:** Longer horizon.  **Goal:** Understand how cells coordinate to build, maintain, and restore complete tissues and organs.

**Measurable outcome:** Reproducible organoids or engineered tissues with functional vascularization, innervation, and clinically meaningful repair.

### 4.13 Human and environmental virology

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Anticipate viral evolution, spillover, immune escape, and treatment resistance while preserving beneficial ecological roles.

**Measurable outcome:** Prospective risk rankings, validated surveillance, and interventions that reduce transmission without unsupported broad restrictions.

## 5. Medicine and public health

### 5.1 Causal clinical inference

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Separate correlation from treatment effect in clinical records, trials, devices, and observational datasets.

**Measurable outcome:** Reproducible estimates with sensitivity analyses and calibration across hospitals and demographic groups.

### 5.2 Precision therapeutics

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Match each patient to treatments based on genetics, history, environment, and likely interactions.

**Measurable outcome:** Lower adverse events and improved outcomes in prospective clinical studies.

### 5.3 Medical AI accountability

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Establish monitoring for dataset shift, bias, failure, security, and silent model degradation after deployment.

**Measurable outcome:** Auditable decisions, incident reporting, rollback plans, and independent post-market evaluation.

### 5.4 Universal, low-cost diagnostics

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Build accurate, portable, inexpensive tests for infectious, metabolic, oncologic, and neurological diseases.

**Measurable outcome:** Sensitivity, specificity, cost, and workflow performance validated in representative real-world settings.

### 5.5 Mental health measurement

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Develop objective, fair measures and decision support without treating every emotion or preference as pathology.

**Measurable outcome:** Improved treatment matching and safety, with no harmful surveillance or coercion.

### 5.6 Reproductive health and maternal outcomes

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Predict and reduce pregnancy complications, maternal mortality, infertility, preterm birth, and unequal access to care.

**Measurable outcome:** Validated risk tools and equitable improvements in prospective maternal and neonatal outcomes.

### 5.7 Rare-disease diagnosis and treatment

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Accelerate interpretation of genomes, phenotypes, and treatment options for diseases affecting small populations.

**Measurable outcome:** Shorter diagnostic timelines and independently verified therapeutic or supportive outcomes.

## 6. Neuroscience, intelligence, and cognition

### 6.1 Brain–machine interfaces

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Restore communication or movement safely and reversibly while preserving privacy and user autonomy.

**Measurable outcome:** Clinically meaningful function, low adverse-event rates, and patient control over data and device operation.

### 6.2 Learning-efficiency theory

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Understand why and how artificial and biological systems learn from few examples, transfer knowledge, and resist forgetting.

**Measurable outcome:** Models that improve sample efficiency and retain previously learned capabilities under distribution shift.

### 6.3 Reliable machine reasoning

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Make language, planning, and scientific agents ground claims in evidence, verify intermediate steps, and know when to abstain.

**Measurable outcome:** Independent benchmarks for factuality, logical validity, tool safety, and uncertainty calibration.

### 6.4 World models

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Build predictive internal models that support planning and cause-and-effect reasoning without merely memorizing surface patterns.

**Measurable outcome:** Strong performance in unseen environments with formal or empirical safety guarantees.

### 6.5 Sleep, memory, and healthy human–AI collaboration

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Understand sleep-dependent repair and memory consolidation, and design AI collaboration that supports rather than displaces human judgment.

**Measurable outcome:** Improved recall and function in long-term studies and demonstrated human retention of decision-making skill.

### 6.6 Generalizable intelligence and transfer

**Timeframe:** Longer horizon.  **Goal:** Build systems that transfer robustly across tasks, bodies, cultures, goals, and previously unseen environments.

**Measurable outcome:** Independent evaluations showing durable competence beyond narrow benchmarks, with explicit uncertainty and safe degradation.

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Reduce uncertainty in regional climate, extreme weather, sea-level rise, tipping points, and human impacts.

**Measurable outcome:** Long-range forecasts that remain skillful under diverse conditions and clearly quantify uncertainty.

### 7.2 Carbon removal and storage

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Develop scalable methods to remove and durably store atmospheric carbon with low ecological and energy costs.

**Measurable outcome:** Measured net removal over full lifecycles, with permanence and leakage independently audited.

### 7.3 Biodiversity monitoring and protection

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Track species, ecosystems, genetic diversity, and extinction risk while supporting local stewardship.

**Measurable outcome:** Timely, field-validated detection of decline and measurable conservation outcomes.

### 7.4 Ocean health and circulation

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Understand warming, acidification, deoxygenation, pollution, fisheries, and deep-ocean currents.

**Measurable outcome:** Better observations and forecasts tied to management decisions and ecosystem recovery.

### 7.5 Critical-resource forecasting

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Predict supply, demand, scarcity, and substitution options for water, phosphorus, lithium, rare elements, and food.

**Measurable outcome:** Robust forecasts across geopolitical and environmental disruptions, with transparent assumptions.

### 7.6 Freshwater security

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Preserve aquifer, river, lake, and watershed function while meeting drinking, agricultural, industrial, and ecological needs.

**Measurable outcome:** Verified availability, quality, and ecosystem-health improvements across drought and flood scenarios.

### 7.7 Arctic and cryosphere systems

**Timeframe:** Near-term priority.  **Goal:** Predict ice-sheet instability, permafrost change, sea ice, methane release, and impacts on coastlines and ecosystems.

**Measurable outcome:** Improved regional forecasts that reproduce observations after initially withheld data and quantify tipping risks.

## 14. Three-year milestone plan

The first three years should prioritize **verification infrastructure**, not dramatic claims:

1. **Prove and reproduce.** Expand machine-assisted theorem verification and require independent reproduction for every claimed result.
2. **Predict before measuring.** Use prospective benchmarks in chemistry, biology, materials, climate, medicine, and physics.
3. **Calibrate uncertainty.** Publish confidence intervals and report negative and inconclusive results.
4. **Control experiments.** Require blinded, randomized, and independently audited tests where appropriate.
5. **Protect people and ecosystems.** Build safety review, privacy, cybersecurity, and environmental impact into each project before scaling.
6. **Share data carefully.** Preserve reproducibility while excluding sensitive personal, security, and dual-use information.
7. **Measure benefit, not hype.** Track clinical outcomes, energy savings, pollution reductions, reproducibility, and human autonomy—not demonstrations alone.
8. **Keep human authority.** Require expert sign-off for proof, diagnosis, deployment, biological editing, high-impact governance, and high-risk physical systems.

## 15. Criteria for calling a problem “solved”

A claim should include:

- An exact formulation and assumptions.
- A proof, experiment, or independently verifiable simulation.
- Controls for bias and systematic error.
- Independent reproduction in different laboratories or environments.
- Uncertainty bounds and negative cases.
- Data and code availability, subject to valid privacy and security restrictions.
- Ethical, ecological, and societal impact assessment.
- A statement of what remains unknown.

No major scientific result should be described as solved solely because an AI generates a plausible answer. AI can accelerate discovery and reduce human error; it cannot replace measurement, verification, or collective scientific judgment.
